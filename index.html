What Is Forex?

Welcome to the world of Forex trading. Forex stands for Foreign Exchange Market — the largest financial market on the planet. Every single day, over $6 trillion is traded globally. That is more than all the world's stock markets combined. In this lecture you will learn exactly what forex is, how currency pairs work, when the market is open, and how to read your very first chart. By the end, you will have the solid foundation every professional trader needs.

What Is Currency Trading?

When you travel to another country, you exchange your home currency for the local one. That exchange — buying one currency while selling another — is the foundation of forex trading. In trading, we do the same thing but with a profit motive: buy a currency when it is cheap and sell it when it becomes expensive.

In forex, currencies are always traded in pairs. You never buy just one currency on its own. When you buy EUR/USD, you are buying Euros and simultaneously selling US Dollars. The exchange rate between the two tells you how much of the second currency you get for one unit of the first.

Example: If EUR/USD is at 1.1000, it means 1 Euro equals 1.10 US Dollars.

Major Currency Pairs

The most traded pairs in the world have the most liquidity, tightest spreads, and most predictable movement:

BUY vs SELL

BUY (Going Long): You believe the currency will go UP in value. You buy it now at a low price and sell it later at a higher price to make profit.

SELL (Going Short): You believe the currency will go DOWN in value. You sell it now at a high price and buy it back later at a lower price to make profit.

The beauty of forex is that you can profit in BOTH directions — whether markets rise or fall.

When To Trade

Forex is open 24 hours a day, 5 days a week. The London–New York overlap (12:00–16:00 GMT) is the best time to trade — highest volume, tightest spreads, strongest price moves. For Nigerian traders that is 1PM–5PM local time.

Candlestick Basics & Market Trends

Candlesticks are the building blocks of every forex chart. Each candle tells a story — who is winning between buyers and sellers, how strongly they are winning, and where price might go next. Master reading candlesticks and you can analyse any market, any timeframe, anywhere in the world. This is one of the most valuable skills you will ever develop as a trader.

Anatomy of a Candlestick

Each candle shows four key pieces of information for a specific time period (1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day, etc.):

PartWhat It Tells You
OPENPrice at start of period
HIGHHighest price reached
LOWLowest price reached
CLOSEPrice at end of period

The body (space between open and close) shows momentum. The wicks (lines above/below body) show rejection — how far price tried to go but was pushed back.

Buyers vs Sellers

Bullish Candle (Green): Close is HIGHER than Open. Buyers won the battle. They pushed price up and held it there. The longer the body, the stronger the buying pressure.

Bearish Candle (Red): Close is LOWER than Open. Sellers won. They pushed price down and held it there. The longer the body, the stronger the selling pressure.

Long wick: The other side fought back. A long upper wick means price tried to go high but sellers rejected it. A long lower wick means price tried to go low but buyers rejected it.

Support & Resistance

Support and Resistance are the most important concepts in all of trading. These are price levels where the market has historically reacted — either bouncing up (support) or falling down (resistance). Think of Support as a floor beneath price that holds it up, and Resistance as a ceiling above price that pushes it back down. Once you master these two ideas, you will have a lifelong edge in every market you ever trade.

What Is Support?

Support is a price level where buyers are strong enough to stop price from falling further. When price approaches support, buyers step in aggressively and push price back up. The more times price has bounced off a support level, the stronger and more reliable that level becomes — because more traders are watching it and reacting to it.

Think of it as a floor beneath the market. Price falls, hits the floor, and bounces back up. Every time it bounces, that floor gets reinforced in traders' minds.

What Is Resistance?

Resistance is a price level where sellers are strong enough to stop price from rising further. When price approaches resistance, sellers step in and push price back down. The more times price has been rejected at a resistance level, the more powerful that level becomes.

Think of it as a ceiling above the market. Price rises, hits the ceiling, and falls back down. Every time it gets rejected, that ceiling becomes stronger.

Trend & Trendlines

Trendlines help you visualise the direction and strength of a market trend. Unlike horizontal Support and Resistance levels that stay fixed at one price, trendlines are dynamic — they move with price and act as moving support or resistance, slanting either up or down. Drawing trendlines correctly is a foundational skill every trader must master before looking for entries.

Uptrend Line (Dynamic Support)

Drawn below price, connecting at least two Higher Lows. Acts as a moving floor beneath price. When price pulls back to the trendline in an uptrend, buyers step in and push it back up. The more times price bounces off the line, the stronger it becomes. You connect the candle wick lows, not the bodies.

Downtrend Line (Dynamic Resistance)

Drawn above price, connecting at least two Lower Highs. Acts as a moving ceiling above price. When price pulls back up to the trendline in a downtrend, sellers step in and push it back down. A break above a downtrend line can signal a potential trend change.

Market Structure — Highs & Lows

Market structure is the backbone of Smart Money Concepts. It answers the most important question in trading: who is in control right now — buyers or sellers? Everything in professional trading begins here. Before looking for entries, before drawing trendlines, before anything else — you identify the structure. Get this right and every other decision becomes dramatically clearer.

Bullish Structure — Buyers In Control

When the market is making Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL), buyers are in full control. Each peak is higher than the last, and each trough is also higher. This is the cleanest and safest environment to look for BUY trades — you are trading WITH the dominant force.

The uptrend remains intact as long as the most recent Higher Low holds. The moment a Higher Low is broken — that is your first warning sign that buyers may be losing control.

Bearish Structure — Sellers In Control

When the market is making Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL), sellers are in full control. Each peak is lower than the last, and each trough is also lower. This is the environment to look for SELL trades.

The downtrend remains intact as long as the most recent Lower High holds. A Lower High being broken to the upside is your first signal that sellers may be losing control — a potential reversal is building.

BULLISH STRUCTURE (Uptrend): BEARISH STRUCTURE (Downtrend): HH LH / \ HH / \ LH / \/ \ / \/ / HH HL / LL / / HL / / LL HL HL LH LH LL Each HH higher than last ✓ Each LH lower than last ✓ Each HL higher than last ✓ Each LL lower than last ✓ → LOOK FOR BUY TRADES → LOOK FOR SELL TRADES
Critical Rule: Never trade against the market structure. If structure is bullish (HH + HL), only look for BUY setups. If structure is bearish (LH + LL), only look for SELL setups. Trading against structure is the #1 cause of avoidable losses for beginner traders.
Key Takeaways
  • HH + HL = Bullish — buyers in control, look for BUY trades only
  • LH + LL = Bearish — sellers in control, look for SELL trades only
  • Structure analysis is the first step before any entry evaluation
  • Never fight the structure — you are one small trader against the entire market
  • A broken Higher Low in an uptrend is your first warning the trend may be changing
Quick Quiz — Lecture 5

EUR/USD is making Lower Highs and Lower Lows. What type of structure is this and what should you trade?

Answer: Bearish Structure (Downtrend). LH + LL = Sellers in control. You should only look for SELL setups in this environment. Looking for BUY trades against this structure means fighting the dominant force — a low-probability, high-risk approach that experienced traders always avoid.

Swing High & Swing Low

Swing Highs and Swing Lows are specific price points that act as liquidity pools. These are places where stop losses and pending orders accumulate. Smart Money — banks, hedge funds, and institutional traders — deliberately targets these levels to collect liquidity before making their real moves. Understanding this single concept gives you a huge edge over the average retail trader.

Swing High

A Swing High is a peak that is higher than the highs on both its left and right sides. It forms where buyers pushed price up but ran out of strength. Above every Swing High, there is accumulated liquidity — stop losses from short sellers, and buy orders from breakout traders waiting above the high.

Smart Money sweeps these levels to grab that liquidity before reversing price downward. This is why you often see sharp fake breakouts above highs followed by immediate reversals.

Swing Low

A Swing Low is a trough that is lower than the lows on both its left and right sides. It forms where sellers pushed price down but ran out of strength. Below every Swing Low, there is accumulated liquidity — stop losses from long traders, and sell orders from breakout traders waiting below the low.

Smart Money sweeps these levels to grab liquidity before reversing price upward. This is the engine behind most major market reversals you will ever see.

Once they have collected the liquidity they need, they push price sharply downward.

Break of Structure (BoS) & Change of Character (ChOCH)

BoS and ChOCH are two of the most powerful tools in Smart Money Concepts. They eliminate guessing — instead of hoping the market moves your way, you wait for the market to confirm its own intention before entering. BoS says the trend is continuing. ChOCH warns the trend may be turning. Together, they tell you when to stay and when to prepare for something new.

Break of Structure (BoS)

BoS occurs when price breaks a key level in the same direction as the current trend. It confirms the trend is healthy and likely to continue. In a downtrend, price breaks below the most recent Low — that is a Bearish BoS. In an uptrend, price breaks above the most recent High — that is a Bullish BoS.

BoS is a continuation signal. After BoS, you wait for price to retest the broken level before entering in the direction of the trend.

Change of Character (ChOCH)

ChOCH occurs when price breaks a key level in the OPPOSITE direction to the current trend. In a downtrend, price breaks above a recent High — Bullish ChOCH. The downtrend may be ending.

Critical: ChOCH is a WARNING, not an entry signal. Do not enter immediately. Wait for additional confirmation — a pullback, a retest, a candlestick pattern. Many traders lose money by entering on ChOCH without confirmation.

Double Top & Double Bottom

Double Top and Double Bottom are two of the most reliable reversal patterns in technical analysis. They form at the end of major trends and signal that the market is exhausted — buyers or sellers have tried twice to push further and both times failed. When you spot these patterns and wait for proper confirmation, they offer high-probability, clearly defined setups with excellent risk-to-reward ratios.

Double Bottom (Bullish Reversal)

A Double Bottom forms at the end of a downtrend. Price makes two equal lows at the same support zone — sellers tried twice to break lower but both times buyers stepped in. This shows sellers are exhausted and buyers are gaining strength.

The Neckline: The resistance level between the two lows. When price breaks ABOVE the neckline, the reversal is confirmed and that is your BUY signal. Wait for a retest of the neckline before entering for the highest probability.

Double Top (Bearish Reversal)

A Double Top forms at the end of an uptrend. Price makes two equal highs at the same resistance zone — buyers tried twice to break higher but both times sellers pushed back. This shows buyers are exhausted and sellers are gaining control.

The Neckline: The support level between the two highs. When price breaks BELOW the neckline, the reversal is confirmed — that is your SELL signal. Wait for a retest before entering.

Key Takeaways
  • Double Bottom = Bullish reversal — two equal lows, break above neckline = BUY
  • Double Top = Bearish reversal — two equal highs, break below neckline = SELL
  • Always wait for the neckline to break AND retest before entering
  • Measure target as the height of the pattern projected from the neckline
  • Combine with BoS, Support/Resistance for higher probability setups
Quick Quiz — Lecture 8

What is the confirmed entry signal for a Double Bottom pattern?

Answer: Breakout above the neckline AND a retest. Simply seeing two equal lows is not enough. You must wait for price to break above the neckline (confirming buyers have taken control) and then ideally retest the neckline as new support before entering BUY.

Risk Management

Risk management is the single most important skill in trading. It is more important than any setup, more important than any indicator, more important than any strategy. Without risk management, even the best strategy will eventually blow your account. A good trader focuses on managing risk — not chasing profits. Profits are the natural result of consistent risk management over time.

⚠️ The Hard Truth: Most traders who lose money do not lose because of bad strategies. They lose because of bad risk management. One single trade without a stop loss can wipe out months of profits in seconds.
The Stop Loss

A Stop Loss is a predetermined price level where your trade automatically closes if the market goes against you. It is the most important tool in your entire trading arsenal — not because it helps you win, but because it limits how much you can lose.

It is non-negotiable. Every single trade you ever place must have a stop loss without exception. Place it at a logical level — below support for BUY trades, above resistance for SELL trades. Never place it at a random number.

The 1-2% Rule

Professional traders never risk more than 1-2% of their total account balance on any single trade. This is the golden rule that keeps professionals in the game.

With a $1,000 account:
• 1% risk = max $10 loss per trade
• If stop loss is 20 pips away: $10 ÷ 20 = $0.50 per pip
• Position size = 0.05 lots

Even 10 losing trades in a row costs only 10% of account — fully recoverable.

RISK MANAGEMENT VISUAL ($1,000 account, 1% risk): TAKE PROFIT ████████████████ → $20 reward (2% of account) | | ← GREEN ZONE (potential profit) | ENTRY ────────────────────── → Your entry price | | ← RED ZONE (protected risk) | STOP LOSS ███████████████████ → $10 max loss (1% of account) Always set BOTH levels BEFORE entering the trade. NEVER move stop loss further away to avoid a loss — that destroys risk management.
The 5 Rules of Risk Management
  • Risk 1-2% per trade — never more than 2% of total account on any single trade
  • Always use a Stop Loss — every trade, every time, zero exceptions
  • Always use a Take Profit — set it before you enter, then let it run
  • Do not overtrade — 1-3 quality trades per day beats 20 random ones
  • Never revenge trade — after a loss, walk away. Come back with a clear head

Trading Psychology

It is often said that trading is 90% psychology and 10% strategy. You can have the best strategy in the world, the best charts, the best tools — but if your emotions control your decisions, you will consistently lose money. Your biggest enemy in the markets is not the market itself. It is the version of you that panics, gets greedy, or tries to win back losses by taking impulsive trades.

The 4 Emotional Enemies

1. Fear — Afraid to enter a valid setup even though all your rules say enter. Closing trades too early. Missing opportunities because of doubt. Solution: Trust your plan. If your criteria are met, execute.

2. Greed — Moving your take profit further and further because you think price will keep going. Increasing lot size after wins. Chasing trades that have already moved. Solution: Set your TP before entry and let it hit.

3. Revenge Trading — Losing a trade and immediately jumping into another to "win it back." This almost always creates bigger losses. Solution: Walk away for at least 15-30 minutes after a loss.

4. Overconfidence — Winning 5 trades in a row and suddenly believing you are invincible. Ignoring your rules. Taking trades that do not meet your criteria. Solution: Stay humble. The market has humbled every trader who became overconfident.

The Professional Mindset

Professional traders do not think "I need to win this trade." They think: "My job is to execute my plan perfectly. The outcome is secondary."

They think in probabilities over a series of trades — not individual outcomes. If their strategy wins 60% of trades over 100 trades with 1:2 R:R, they trust the numbers and follow the plan regardless of each individual result.

Before every single trade, ask yourself: Is the trend clear? Is my setup valid? Where is my stop loss? What is my R:R? Am I entering because of a real setup or because of emotion?

Key Takeaways
  • Fear, greed, revenge, overconfidence are your four biggest trading enemies
  • Discipline consistently beats emotion — every single time, over the long run
  • Losses are part of the business — even professionals lose trades regularly
  • Walk away after any loss — never trade when emotional
  • The goal is consistent execution of your plan, not winning every individual trade

Candlestick Types — The Complete Guide

In Lecture 2 you learned the basics of candlesticks. In this lecture, we go deeper. Every candlestick type has a specific meaning and tells you something specific about market psychology at that exact moment. Learning to read each type fluently is like learning to understand the language the market speaks — and once you do, you will never look at a chart the same way again.

The Main Candle Types

Long Bullish Candle: Very small or no wicks, large green body. Buyers were completely dominant the entire period. Very strong continuation signal in an uptrend. Watch for these during the London-New York overlap for strong momentum trades.

Long Bearish Candle: Very small or no wicks, large red body. Sellers were completely dominant. Very strong continuation signal in a downtrend. Often appears on news releases or at key resistance breaks.

Doji: Open and close are almost equal — tiny or no body, wicks on both sides. Represents complete indecision — buyers and sellers fought all period and neither won. Do not trade on a Doji alone. Wait for the next candle to confirm direction.

More Candle Types

Bullish Pinbar (Hammer): Small body at the top, very long lower wick. Sellers tried to push price down strongly but buyers overpowered them completely and pushed price back up. The longer the wick, the stronger the rejection. Find these at support levels for high-probability BUY signals.

Bearish Pinbar (Shooting Star): Small body at the bottom, very long upper wick. Buyers tried to push price up strongly but sellers overwhelmed them completely. Find these at resistance levels for high-probability SELL signals.

Spinning Top: Small body with long wicks on both sides. Similar to Doji — both sides fought and neither won convincingly. Signals indecision. Wait for confirmation from the next candle before acting.

Liquidity & Liquidity Sweeps

Liquidity is one of the most important — and most misunderstood — concepts in forex trading. Most retail traders think the market moves randomly or in response to news. Professional Smart Money traders know the truth: the market moves to take liquidity. Once you understand this, you will start to see charts completely differently — and you will stop being the person who gets swept out of trades right before price moves.

What Is Liquidity?

Liquidity is where large concentrations of buy orders, sell orders, stop losses, and pending orders are clustered. These areas attract price like a magnet because institutional traders need large amounts of liquidity to fill their massive positions without moving the market against themselves.

Where does liquidity sit? Above every high (where sellers have stop losses and buyers have orders), and below every low (where buyers have stop losses and sellers have orders). The bigger and more obvious the high or low, the more liquidity sits around it.

The Liquidity Sweep

A Liquidity Sweep is when price briefly moves beyond a key level — above a high or below a low — triggering all the orders there, then immediately reverses in the opposite direction.

This is Smart Money collecting the liquidity they need to fill their real positions. After the sweep, they push price in their true intended direction — and that is where your trade opportunity lies. You enter AFTER the sweep, on confirmation of the reversal, not before.

Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

A Fair Value Gap is one of the most useful entry tools in Smart Money Concepts. It appears when price moves so fast in one direction that it leaves a gap — an area of price that was never properly traded. Think of it as an inefficiency the market is obligated to return and fix. Price returns to fill these gaps remarkably often, making them high-precision entry zones when combined with your other tools.

What Creates an FVG?

An FVG is a 3-candle pattern. A large impulsive candle (Candle 2) moves so fast that it creates a gap between Candle 1 and Candle 3. In a Bullish FVG, Candle 3's LOW is higher than Candle 1's HIGH — there is untradeable space between them. In a Bearish FVG, Candle 3's HIGH is lower than Candle 1's LOW.

This gap represents price inefficiency — orders that were never filled because the market moved too fast. Price often returns to these zones to fill those orders before continuing in the original direction.

How to Trade FVGs

The simplest approach: identify an FVG in the direction of your higher-timeframe trend, mark the gap zone, and wait for price to return to it. When price enters the FVG, look for a confirmation candle (engulfing, hammer, or BoS) and enter your trade. Your stop loss goes below the FVG (for bullish) or above it (for bearish). Your target is the next significant support or resistance level.

FVGs work best when combined with Order Blocks, BoS, and key support/resistance levels — multiple confirmations at the same zone create very high-probability setups.

Order Blocks (OB)

Order Blocks are the most powerful entry tool in Smart Money Concepts. They represent the exact price zones where large institutions — banks, hedge funds, and market makers — placed their initial orders before causing a major move. When price returns to these zones, institutions often add to their positions, creating highly reliable bounces. Great traders do not chase price. They wait for price to return to them at quality zones — and Order Blocks are the highest quality zones on any chart.

What Is an Order Block?

An Order Block is the last opposite-coloured candle before a strong impulsive move. When a major institution places a large buy or sell order, they cannot fill the entire position at once without moving the market against themselves. So they fill part of it, push price away to create a move, then wait for price to return to fill the rest of their order at the same zone.

Bullish OB: The last red (bearish) candle before a strong move UP. Institutions placed buy orders here. When price returns, they add to their long positions — creating a bounce.

Bearish OB: The last green (bullish) candle before a strong move DOWN. Institutions placed sell orders here. When price returns, they add to their short positions — creating a rejection.

How to Identify an Order Block

Step 1: Find a strong impulsive move — a large candle (or series of candles) moving strongly in one direction. This is the institutional push.

Step 2: Look at the candle immediately BEFORE that strong move began. The last opposite-coloured candle is your Order Block.

Step 3: Mark the HIGH and LOW of that candle. That price zone is your Order Block zone.

Step 4: Wait for price to return to that zone. Look for confirmation (BoS, ChOCH, engulfing candle).

Step 5: Enter only after confirmation. Stop Loss below OB (bullish) or above OB (bearish).

Final Quiz — Lecture 20

What exactly is a Bullish Order Block and how do you identify it on a chart?

Answer: A Bullish Order Block is the last RED (bearish) candle BEFORE a strong upward move. To identify it: find a strong impulsive move upward, look at the candle immediately before that move started, and mark its high and low as your order block zone. When price returns to that zone, institutions often add to their buy positions — creating a high-probability bounce with tight stop losses and large targets.
Reference Guide

Master Glossary

Every important term from all 20 lectures in one place. Bookmark this as your quick reference while you practise.

BoS — Break of Structure
Price breaks a key level in the SAME direction as the trend. Confirms the trend continues.
ChOCH — Change of Character
Price breaks a key level in the OPPOSITE direction. Signals a possible trend reversal is building.
Support
A price level where buyers step in and stop price from falling. Acts as a floor.
Resistance
A price level where sellers step in and stop price from rising. Acts as a ceiling.
HH — Higher High
A peak higher than the previous peak. Part of bullish market structure.
HL — Higher Low
A trough higher than the previous trough. Confirms the uptrend is intact.
LH — Lower High
A peak lower than the previous peak. Part of bearish market structure.
LL — Lower Low
A trough lower than the previous trough. Confirms the downtrend is intact.
Swing High
A high that is higher than highs on both sides. A liquidity level above price.
Swing Low
A low that is lower than lows on both sides. A liquidity level below price.
FVG — Fair Value Gap
A 3-candle price imbalance where the market moved too fast. Price often returns to fill it.
Order Block (OB)
Last opposite candle before a strong move. Institutional zone where price often reverses.
R:R — Risk to Reward
Ratio comparing your potential loss to your potential profit. Minimum: 1:2.
SL — Stop Loss
A level that automatically closes your trade to limit losses. Non-negotiable on every trade.
TP — Take Profit
A level that automatically closes your trade when your profit target is reached.
MA — Moving Average
An indicator showing average price over a period. Used as a trend filter.
Liquidity Sweep
When price briefly moves above/below a key level to collect stop losses, then reverses.
Bullish Engulfing
A green candle that completely engulfs the previous red candle. Strongest 2-candle buy signal.
